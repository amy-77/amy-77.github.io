About

Algorithm-first research for long-context intelligence.

I am a PhD candidate at Universite Paris Cite, advised by Prof. Themis Palpanas. My current direction is to design sparse attention mechanisms that are learnable, stable, and useful during training, so long-context models can reason with less redundant computation while preserving the information they need.

Before my PhD, I studied Mathematics at Harbin Institute of Technology, Shenzhen, worked on pattern mining, and collaborated with teams at HKUST, HKUST(GZ), and City University of Hong Kong on learned indexes, data selection, and LLM-driven optimization. These projects shaped how I think about sparse structure, selection objectives, and data-dependent algorithms.

Sparse Attention

From sparse retrieval signals toward trainable sparse attention.

ICML 2026 arXiv v3, May 2026

ParisKV: Fast and Drift-Robust KV-Cache Retrieval for Long-Context LLMs

Yanlin Qi, Xinhang Chen, Huiqiang Jiang, Qitong Wang, Botao Peng, Themis Palpanas

ParisKV is an algorithmic step in this direction: it studies how to select the useful part of a long attention history under distribution drift, using collision-based candidate selection and calibrated reranking.

  • Accepted to the 43rd International Conference on Machine Learning.
  • Connects attention sparsity, Top-k selection, and robustness under long generation.
  • Up to 2.8x higher decoding throughput than full attention within its runnable range.
  • 17x and 44x lower million-token decode latency than MagicPIG and PQCache.
